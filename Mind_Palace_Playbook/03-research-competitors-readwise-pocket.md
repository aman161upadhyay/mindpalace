# Research — The Read-Later & Highlighting Market: What Worked, What Died, and Where the Gap Is

> **Executive summary.** The "save-and-resurface" category (read-later + web highlighting + personal knowledge management) just went through a brutal consolidation. **Pocket — 35M users — was shut down by Mozilla on 8 July 2025**, and **Omnivore was acqui-hired by ElevenLabs in late 2024 and deleted all user data**. That displaced millions of users and proved the category's central lesson: *features are cheap, but trust and durability are the moat.* The survivor that makes money — **Readwise (~$5M+ ARR, ~11 people, bootstrapped, profitable)** — wins not on features but on **the daily-resurfacing habit loop + breadth of integrations + reliability**. Almost every product in this space dies of the same disease: the **"highlight graveyard"** — users save constantly and revisit nothing, so the product feels worthless and churns. Mind Palace's wedge for researchers/academics is the one thing none of the incumbents do well: **synthesis across sources** (turn scattered highlights into connected, queryable, citable knowledge), not just storage. Verified May 2026.

## 2. Why Pocket and Omnivore died — and what it teaches you

**Pocket (35M users, killed by its own owner).** Mozilla's stated reason: "the way people browse the web is changing" and it wanted to refocus resources. The deeper truth: Pocket was a *feature inside someone else's strategy*, never a standalone business with its own P&L. When it stopped being strategically useful to Mozilla, 35M users' libraries were deleted on a corporate calendar. **Lesson: if the product isn't a business, the users are a liability, not an asset.** Mind Palace must be a real business (revenue that covers its own costs) so it never depends on a parent's whim.

**Omnivore (open-source, beloved, gone in a month).** It was free, had no revenue, ran on burn, and the team was acqui-hired by ElevenLabs in Oct 2024. The service went dark in November 2024 and **all user data was deleted**. The open-source code survived; the *hosted service people actually used* did not. **Lesson: "free + open source + VC-ish ambition" with no monetization is the most fragile structure in this category.** A small paid business is *more* durable for users than a free one.

**The combined signal to your market:** millions of read-later/highlight users in 2025 learned the hard way that **durability and data-portability matter more than features.** This is the single best wedge available to a new entrant in 2026: *"We're a sustainable paid business; your knowledge is yours, exportable any time, and we won't disappear."* Lead with it.

## 3. Why Readwise is defensible and profitable (despite being "expensive" and "feature-choked")

Readwise is the case study to copy. It is not the prettiest or cheapest. It wins on three structural moats:

1. **The habit loop is the product.** Readwise's core is the **daily resurfacing email/app** built on spaced repetition. This converts a passive archive into a recurring reason to open the app. Every other app stores; Readwise *re-shows*. That daily touch is what justifies a subscription and crushes churn. **Mind Palace already has the daily email digest — this is your most important asset. Lean into it hard.**
2. **Integration breadth = switching cost.** Kindle, Apple Books, Instapaper, Twitter, podcasts, OCR of physical books, exports to Notion/Obsidian/Roam. Once a user's highlights from 6 sources flow into Readwise and out to their note system, leaving means rebuilding their whole pipeline. **The accumulated, connected data is the moat — not the highlight button.**
3. **Reliability + reputation in a graveyard of dead apps.** After Pocket/Omnivore, "they've been around since 2017, profitable, won't die" is a genuine selling point.

**Complaints to exploit:** Readwise is ["expensive" ($120/yr, steep outside the US)](https://readwise.io/pricing/reader) and Reader's UI is widely described as **"choked with features"** and intimidating to new users. Matter wins design-conscious users precisely because it's simpler. **There is room for a cleaner, cheaper, more focused product — especially one aimed at a specific audience (researchers/academics) rather than "everyone."**

## 4. The "highlight graveyard" — the #1 reason these products churn

The dominant complaint across Reddit/HN/reviews for *every* product in this category: **"I save tons of stuff and never look at it again."** Saving is frictionless and feels productive; retrieval and synthesis are hard and rare. An app that is only a save button becomes a junk drawer, then gets uninstalled.

**How winners fight it (and what Mind Palace should do):**

| Tactic | Who does it | Mind Palace action |
|---|---|---|
| Daily resurfacing email/feed | Readwise (core), Matter | ✅ Already built — make it *great*, not an afterthought |
| Spaced repetition / review queue | Readwise | Add a "Review" mode in the dashboard, not just email |
| AI summarization & Q&A over your library | Glasp, Liner, Recall (emerging) | **Biggest opportunity — see §6** |
| Auto-tagging / auto-topics | Mem, Mind Palace (has metadataTags) | ✅ Already have auto-topics — surface them more |
| Connections between related highlights | Heptabase, Obsidian graph, Recall | The researcher killer feature — "you highlighted something related 3 weeks ago" |
| Export to the user's note system | Readwise (Notion/Obsidian/Roam) | High priority — reduces lock-in fear *and* increases stickiness |

## 5. What people LOVE vs DON'T LOVE (themes from reviews)

**Loved:**
- Frictionless capture (one shortcut/click, works everywhere) — table stakes.
- The daily resurfacing habit ("it's the only app that makes me actually re-read my notes").
- Clean, calm, fast UI (Matter, MyMind, Raindrop praised here).
- Data ownership & export (post-Pocket, this is now a *feature*, not a checkbox).
- Cheap, fair pricing (Raindrop's ~$3/mo is repeatedly cited as a reason people happily pay).

**Disliked / churn drivers:**
- **Highlight graveyard** (see §4) — the universal one.
- **Price + steep learning curve** (Readwise Reader's "too many features").
- **Mobile gaps** (Glasp has no strong mobile app — a constant complaint; Matter is iOS-only — alienates Android/desktop users).
- **Pivots that betray the core use case** (Liner pivoted to AI search and alienated highlighters; lesson: don't abandon your power users for a trend).
- **Ads / clutter** (Weava).
- **Account-required friction & privacy fear** for browser extensions (covered in [04-research-chrome-extension-businesses.md](04-research-chrome-extension-businesses.md)).
- **Fear the service will vanish** (now acute post-Pocket/Omnivore).

## 6. The gap Mind Palace should own: synthesis for researchers/academics

Mind Palace's stated target — researchers, scientists, academics, knowledge workers, starting at Harvard/HBS — maps onto a real, underserved gap. The academic workflow research is blunt: **tools like Zotero are used as "just storage" — save a paper, attach a PDF, add a tag, move on** — and the acknowledged weakness is that **"note-taking works one-to-one with a PDF, but writing is about synthesizing ideas across many sources."** ([FOSS Academic Zotero deep dive](https://fossacademic.tech/2022/10/05/deepdivezotero.html), [Better Notes for Zotero](https://scite.ai/blog/best-zotero-plugins-for-researchers))

That is the wedge. The winning positioning is **not** "another read-later app." It is:

> **"Mind Palace turns everything you read on the web into one searchable, connected, citable knowledge base — and resurfaces it so you actually use it."**

**Concrete differentiators to build (prioritized):**

1. **AI Q&A / synthesis over your own highlights** ("What have I saved about X?", "Summarize my highlights on transformer scaling laws", "Draft a lit-review paragraph from these 8 highlights with citations"). This is the feature that converts a graveyard into a research assistant. *This is the single biggest product bet.*
2. **Source-aware capture + citation export** — capture the page title, author, URL, date (Mind Palace already stores `sourceUrl`, `pageTitle`, `domain`); add **export to BibTeX / RIS / APA / MLA / Notion / Obsidian / Markdown**. Researchers need citations, not just text.
3. **Connections / "related highlights"** — surface semantically related saves across sources. Solves the synthesis gap directly.
4. **PDF & academic-paper highlighting** — researchers live in PDFs (arXiv, journals). Web-only capture misses half their reading.
5. **Collections / projects** — group highlights by paper, project, or course. Critical for lit reviews and dissertations.
6. **Keep it calm and focused** — explicitly *not* the "feature-choked" Reader. Beat Readwise on focus, beat Glasp on mobile/reliability, beat Pocket/Omnivore on "we won't die."

## 8. Prioritized recommendations (product & positioning)

1. **Position as a research/knowledge tool for academics & knowledge workers, not a generic read-later app.** Narrow audience = sharper marketing and word-of-mouth (your HBS network is the ideal beachhead).
2. **Make the daily resurfacing email world-class.** It's your retention engine and your single biggest existing asset. (Currently uses Resend sandbox domain — fix that first; see infra doc.)
3. **Build AI synthesis/Q&A over highlights** as the flagship paid feature — it's the antidote to the highlight graveyard and the thing no incumbent does well for researchers.
4. **Ship export (Markdown/Notion/Obsidian/BibTeX) early.** Counterintuitively, *reducing* lock-in builds trust post-Pocket and increases adoption; the real lock-in is the accumulated connected data + habit.
5. **Lead marketing with durability & data ownership** — "a sustainable paid product that won't disappear and whose data is always yours." This directly answers the Pocket/Omnivore trauma.
6. **Add PDF/paper capture and Collections** to serve the academic workflow Zotero leaves unsolved.
7. **Don't over-feature.** Beat Readwise Reader by being the calm, focused alternative.
